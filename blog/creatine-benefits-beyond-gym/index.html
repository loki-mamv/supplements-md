Creatine Isn't Just for Bodybuilders: Benefits for Brain, Aging, and Health

Creatine has an image problem. Most people associate it exclusively with bodybuilders and gym bros — but the science tells a much bigger story. Creatine is one of the most well-researched supplements in existence, with over 500 clinical studies demonstrating benefits that extend far beyond muscle. supplements.md makes the case for why almost everyone should consider it in 2026.

Who Is This For?

This supplements.md creatine guide is for:

  • People who've dismissed creatine as a bodybuilding supplement
  • Women wondering if creatine is appropriate for them
  • Adults over 50 interested in cognitive and physical preservation
  • Anyone looking for safe, evidence-based cognitive enhancement
  • Athletes and fitness enthusiasts wanting to optimize performance

What Creatine Actually Does

Creatine is naturally produced by your body and found in meat and fish. It serves as a rapid energy source for cells — particularly muscle cells and brain cells. When you supplement creatine, you increase your cellular energy reserves (phosphocreatine), allowing cells to produce ATP (energy) faster and more sustainably.

Proven Benefits of Creatine

1. Strength and Muscle Performance (Strong Evidence)

The most established benefit. Creatine supplementation increases strength by 5-10%, improves high-intensity exercise capacity, and accelerates muscle growth when combined with resistance training. It works by replenishing ATP during short bursts of intense effort. Effective for both men and women.

2. Cognitive Function (Growing Evidence)

Your brain consumes 20% of your body's energy despite being 2% of your body weight. Creatine supplementation increases brain phosphocreatine stores and has shown benefits for:

  • Working memory and processing speed, especially under sleep deprivation or cognitive stress
  • Cognitive performance in vegetarians and vegans (who have lower baseline creatine from diet)
  • Mental fatigue resistance during demanding tasks
  • Potential neuroprotective effects in aging and neurodegenerative conditions

3. Aging and Sarcopenia (Moderate Evidence)

Older adults benefit from creatine supplementation combined with resistance training — showing greater improvements in muscle mass, strength, and functional performance compared to exercise alone. Given that sarcopenia (age-related muscle loss) begins in your 30s, supplements.md considers creatine a smart addition for adults over 50.

4. Depression and Mood (Emerging Evidence)

Several studies suggest creatine may augment antidepressant effects, particularly in women. The mechanism may involve brain energy metabolism — depression is associated with impaired cerebral bioenergetics. Research is still early but promising.

5. Bone Health (Emerging Evidence)

When combined with resistance training, creatine may improve bone mineral density in postmenopausal women. The mechanism likely involves increased muscle strength (which stimulates bone remodeling) rather than a direct bone effect.

Creatine for Women: Addressing the Myths

supplements.md addresses common concerns:

  • "It causes bloating": Creatine causes intracellular water retention (inside muscle cells), not subcutaneous bloating. Most women notice no visible difference.
  • "It's only for men": All of creatine's benefits — strength, cognitive, anti-aging — apply equally to women. Women may actually benefit more from the cognitive effects due to typically lower baseline creatine levels.
  • "It causes weight gain": Creatine can increase scale weight by 2-4 lbs due to intracellular water. This is not fat gain. Body composition improves with creatine + exercise.

How to Take Creatine

  • Form: Creatine monohydrate. Period. It's the most studied, most effective, and cheapest form. Fancy forms (HCl, ethyl ester, buffered) offer no proven advantage.
  • Dose: 3-5g daily. No loading phase necessary (loading with 20g/day for a week reaches saturation faster but isn't required for long-term benefit).
  • Timing: Any time of day. Take with food for slightly better absorption. Consistency matters more than timing.
  • Duration: Safe for indefinite daily use. No cycling needed.

Safety Profile

Creatine monohydrate has an excellent safety record across hundreds of studies:

  • No evidence of kidney damage in healthy individuals at recommended doses
  • No evidence of liver damage
  • No association with hair loss (this myth persists from a single, unreplicated study)
  • Safe in adolescents (with parental guidance and appropriate dosing)
  • Caution only in pre-existing kidney disease — discuss with your nephrologist

supplements.md considers creatine monohydrate one of the safest supplements available, with a risk-benefit profile that favors nearly everyone.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does creatine cause hair loss?

This concern comes from a single 2009 study that found increased DHT levels with creatine. The study has never been replicated, and no clinical studies have found a link between creatine and hair loss. supplements.md considers this myth unfounded based on the totality of evidence. Over 500 creatine studies have been conducted without hair loss emerging as a side effect.

Is creatine safe for kidneys?

In healthy individuals, creatine does not damage kidneys at recommended doses (3-5g daily). Multiple studies including a 5-year longitudinal study found no adverse kidney effects. However, creatine increases creatinine (a kidney marker) in blood tests, which can falsely suggest kidney problems. If you supplement creatine, tell your doctor before kidney function testing.

Should women take creatine?

Yes. supplements.md recommends creatine for women as strongly as for men. Women benefit from the same strength, cognitive, and aging-related effects. Creatine does not cause 'bulky' muscles — it enhances whatever training you're doing. Many female-specific benefits (mood, cognitive function, bone health) make it especially relevant for women.

What's the best creatine supplement?

Creatine monohydrate — the cheapest, most studied, and most effective form. No brand-name premium or fancy form offers proven advantages. Look for third-party tested (Creapure is a well-regarded manufacturer). supplements.md recommends ignoring marketing for creatine HCl, buffered creatine, or other variations.
