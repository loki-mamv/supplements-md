Are Expired Vitamins Safe to Take?

September 24, 2026 6 min read By thrive.md Editorial Team
Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida

The short answer: Most expired vitamins and minerals are not known to turn toxic. The main risk is that they get weaker, so you may get less than the label says. Fish oil and probiotics are the exceptions to watch: fish oil can go rancid, and probiotic bacteria die off over time. If a product smells off, has changed color or texture, or you rely on it for a medical reason, replace it.

Do supplements even have to carry an expiration date?

No. This surprises many people. The FDA's manufacturing rules for dietary supplements do not require an expiration date, a shelf date, or a "best if used by" date. The agency's compliance guide for supplement makers answers that question with a plain "No."

The same guide says a company should have data to support any date it chooses to print. So a date on the bottle is the maker's own claim, backed by its own stability testing. It is not a date set or checked by the FDA before sale. That fits the wider system, where supplements do not get FDA approval before they reach shelves. Our guide to how supplements are regulated covers that in more detail.

Medicines work differently. Prescription and over-the-counter drugs carry expiration dates, and the FDA's advice is simple: do not use expired medicines. The agency says expired products can lose strength or change chemically, and weak antibiotics can fail to treat an infection. If your "vitamin" is a prescription product or carries a Drug Facts label, follow the medicine rule.

What happens to a vitamin after the date passes?

Nothing dramatic happens on the date itself. The date marks the point to which the maker expects the product to meet its label. After that, the amount of each nutrient may keep falling slowly.

Federal labeling rules set a floor for that label. Under 21 CFR 101.36, supplements are judged by the same nutrient standards used for fortified foods. Added vitamins and minerals must be present at no less than the amount declared on the label. The rule also allows "reasonable excesses" under good manufacturing practice. Makers often add extra of fragile vitamins so the product still meets its label late in its shelf life.

That means a vitamin a few months past its date may still hold close to its label amount. A vitamin that is years old, or one kept in heat and humidity, may not. You cannot tell by looking at a pill how much is left.

Minerals such as calcium, zinc, and magnesium are stable elements, so the mineral itself does not "wear out" the way some vitamins do. The tablet around it can still absorb moisture, crumble, or break down.

Which supplements lose strength fastest?

Not all nutrients age at the same rate. Research points to a few that are more fragile than the rest:

  • Vitamin C and thiamin (B1). In a Purdue University study, researchers stored vitamin powders at controlled humidity levels for up to 12 weeks. Vitamin C and thiamin broke down much faster once humidity rose high enough for the powder to start dissolving in moisture from the air. Vitamin C also sped up thiamin loss in mixed blends. Pyridoxine (B6) was the least affected.
  • Powdered vitamin C with anticaking agents. A follow-up study found that storage humidity and time both affected vitamin C stability. None of the anticaking agents tested improved its chemical stability.
  • Probiotics. These are live microbes, and they can die during storage. The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements advises looking for products that list colony forming units (CFU) at the end of shelf life, not at the time of manufacture. An expired probiotic is unlikely to harm a healthy person, but it may hold far fewer live organisms. Our probiotics evidence review covers what the live count means.
  • Fish oil. Omega-3 fats oxidize when exposed to air, heat, and light. That is the one common supplement where "old" can mean "spoiled," covered in the next section.

Should you worry about old or rancid fish oil?

More than other supplements, yes. Omega-3 fats break down into compounds that cause the rancid smell and off flavor of oxidized oil. Researchers measure this with peroxide and anisidine values.

A 2015 study of 32 fish oil products sold in New Zealand found that 83% exceeded the recommended peroxide level. Only 8% met all the international oxidation limits. The authors also found the best-before date was a poor marker of quality. A later 2016 analysis of 10 products in Australia and New Zealand, using accredited labs, found all 10 met the peroxide limit. The two teams disagree about test methods, so the true rate is unsettled.

What happens when people take oxidized fish oil is also unsettled. A 2015 review states that the health effects of consuming oxidized fish oil supplements are not yet established. Given that uncertainty, a practical rule makes sense. If a capsule smells strongly fishy or sour when you cut it open, throw the bottle out. Our omega-3 and fish oil guide explains what to look for when buying.

When should you throw an old supplement away?

A few weeks or months past the date, a basic multivitamin stored in a cool, dry place is usually a potency question, not a safety one. Replace it sooner in these cases:

  • It smells rancid, sour, or unusual.
  • Tablets have changed color, softened, stuck together, or crumbled.
  • Gummies have melted, hardened, or clumped.
  • It is a probiotic, and the live count matters to you.
  • You take it for a diagnosed need, such as a deficiency your clinician is treating. You want the full dose, so use an in-date product.
  • It is a medicine, not a supplement. The FDA advises against using any expired medicine.

How should you store supplements and get rid of old ones?

Storage decides how close a product stays to its label. The FDA advises keeping most medicines in a cool, dry place such as a dresser drawer or closet shelf. It warns that a damp bathroom cabinet can weaken products even before their date. The same logic fits supplements, given the humidity research above. Keep lids tight, leave any drying packet in the bottle, and follow label directions such as "refrigerate after opening."

Old iron pills need special care. Federal law requires iron supplements in tablet or capsule form to carry this warning: "Accidental overdose of iron-containing products is a leading cause of fatal poisoning in children under 6." That risk does not expire, so keep old iron products out of reach until you discard them.

For disposal, the FDA's medicine disposal guidance says take-back sites and mail-back envelopes are the best option. If neither is available, mix the pills with dirt, cat litter, or used coffee grounds. Seal the mixture in a plastic bag and put it in the trash. Do not flush anything unless it is on the FDA flush list.

The bottom line

Expired vitamins are rarely dangerous, but they can be weaker than the label says. The FDA does not require supplement expiration dates, so any date you see is the maker's own estimate. Heat and humidity shorten a product's life more than the calendar does. Treat fish oil and probiotics with the most caution, and replace anything that smells or looks wrong. Frequently Asked Questions

Can expired vitamins make you sick?

Standard vitamins and minerals are not known to become toxic after their date. The usual change is a slow loss of strength. Rancid fish oil and products that have absorbed moisture are the main cases where you should throw the bottle out rather than finish it.

How long are vitamins good after the expiration date?

No rule gives a safe number of months, because the FDA does not set supplement dates and each maker tests its own products. Storage matters a lot: humidity and heat speed up breakdown of vitamin C and thiamin in lab studies. A product kept cool and dry will hold up better than one kept in a steamy bathroom.

Are expired prenatal vitamins still worth taking?

During pregnancy the goal is a reliable dose, especially of folic acid. Because an expired product may contain less than its label, it makes sense to replace it with an in-date bottle. Ask your clinician or pharmacist if you are unsure.

Does refrigerating vitamins make them last longer?

Only if the label says to refrigerate, as some probiotics and oils do. For most tablets, a cool, dry cabinet is the target. Taking a cold bottle in and out of the fridge can draw moisture inside, and moisture is what degrades many vitamins.

Can I throw old vitamins in the trash?

Yes, if no take-back option is available. The FDA suggests mixing pills with coffee grounds, dirt, or cat litter and sealing them in a bag first. Keep old iron supplements away from children until they are gone.
