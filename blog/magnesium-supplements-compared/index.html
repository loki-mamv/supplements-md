Magnesium Supplements Compared: Glycinate, Citrate, Oxide, and More

Walk into the supplement aisle and you'll find a dozen types of magnesium — glycinate, citrate, oxide, threonate, taurate, malate, and more. They're not interchangeable. Each form has different absorption, different effects, and different best uses. supplements.md compares every form so you can choose the right one for your needs in 2026.

Who Is This For?

This supplements.md magnesium guide is for:

  • People taking magnesium oxide who aren't seeing benefits
  • Anyone wanting to try magnesium for sleep, anxiety, or muscle cramps
  • People confused by the many magnesium options
  • Those wondering if they're magnesium deficient

Why Magnesium Matters

Magnesium is involved in 300+ enzymatic reactions — muscle contraction, nerve function, blood sugar regulation, blood pressure control, and bone health. An estimated 50% of Americans don't get adequate magnesium from food. Soil depletion, processed food diets, and increased stress all contribute to widespread insufficiency.

Every Form of Magnesium Compared

Magnesium Glycinate (Bisglycinate)

Best for: Sleep, anxiety, general supplementation

Magnesium bound to glycine, a calming amino acid. High bioavailability (well-absorbed). Doesn't cause digestive issues. The calming properties come from both the magnesium and the glycine. supplements.md considers this the best all-around magnesium supplement for most people.

Dose: 200-400mg elemental magnesium before bed.

Magnesium Citrate

Best for: Constipation, general supplementation

Good absorption. Has an osmotic laxative effect — draws water into the intestines. Excellent for people who tend toward constipation. Can cause loose stools in sensitive individuals. Widely available and affordable.

Dose: 200-400mg elemental magnesium.

Magnesium L-Threonate

Best for: Cognitive function, brain health

The only form shown to cross the blood-brain barrier and increase brain magnesium levels. Studied specifically for cognitive enhancement and memory. Magtein is the branded version. More expensive than other forms. Lower elemental magnesium per capsule means you take more pills.

Dose: 1,500-2,000mg of magnesium L-threonate (provides ~144mg elemental magnesium).

Magnesium Taurate

Best for: Heart health, blood pressure

Magnesium bound to taurine. Both components support cardiovascular function. Some evidence for blood pressure and heart rhythm benefits. Good absorption, gentle on the stomach.

Dose: 200-400mg elemental magnesium.

Magnesium Malate

Best for: Energy, muscle pain, fibromyalgia

Magnesium bound to malic acid (involved in energy production). May help with chronic fatigue and muscle pain. Good absorption. Less sedating than glycinate — better for daytime use.

Dose: 200-400mg elemental magnesium.

Magnesium Oxide

Best for: Nothing, really

The most common and cheapest form — and the worst. Only about 4% bioavailability (you absorb very little). Primarily acts as a laxative. supplements.md does not recommend magnesium oxide for supplementation. If you're currently taking this form and not seeing benefits, switch to glycinate or citrate.

Magnesium Chloride

Best for: Topical use (lotions, bath salts)

Available as topical sprays, lotions, and Epsom salt alternatives. Evidence for transdermal absorption is limited, but many people report muscle relaxation and cramp relief from topical use. Also available orally with decent absorption.

How Much Magnesium Do You Need?

RDA: 400-420mg/day for adult men, 310-320mg/day for adult women. Many experts recommend 400-600mg total from food and supplements combined. supplements.md recommends supplementing with 200-400mg elemental magnesium if your diet is typical (most people don't eat enough dark leafy greens, nuts, seeds, and whole grains).

Signs of Magnesium Deficiency

  • Muscle cramps and spasms (especially at night)
  • Poor sleep quality
  • Anxiety and restlessness
  • Fatigue despite adequate sleep
  • Eye twitching
  • Headaches
  • Constipation
  • Heart palpitations

When to Take Magnesium

  • For sleep: Glycinate 30-60 minutes before bed
  • For general health: Any time, preferably with food
  • For constipation: Citrate in the morning or before bed
  • For energy: Malate in the morning (avoid before bed)

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the best magnesium for sleep?

Magnesium glycinate (bisglycinate). The glycine component has its own calming and sleep-promoting properties, and the magnesium itself promotes muscle relaxation and GABA activity. Take 200-400mg about 30-60 minutes before bed. supplements.md considers this the single best magnesium form for sleep improvement.

Why is magnesium oxide so common if it's poorly absorbed?

It's the cheapest to manufacture and contains the highest percentage of elemental magnesium per pill (60% vs 14-16% for glycinate). But only about 4% is actually absorbed, so you're mostly paying for magnesium that passes through you. supplements.md recommends switching to glycinate or citrate for meaningful supplementation.

Can you take too much magnesium?

Excess oral magnesium typically causes diarrhea before reaching dangerous levels — your body eliminates the excess through the digestive tract. However, people with kidney disease should be cautious as their kidneys can't excrete excess magnesium efficiently. The UL for supplemental magnesium is 350mg/day, though many people safely take more.

Should I take magnesium every day?

Yes, if your diet doesn't provide adequate magnesium (most don't). Unlike some supplements, magnesium is used daily by your body and doesn't accumulate to dangerous levels in healthy individuals. Taking 200-400mg of a well-absorbed form daily is safe and beneficial for most adults.
