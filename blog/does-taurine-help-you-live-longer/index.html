Does Taurine Help You Live Longer?

October 20, 2026 6 min read By thrive.md Editorial Team
Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida

The short answer: No one knows yet. A 2023 study in Science found that taurine extended average lifespan in middle-aged mice by 10% to 12%. No study has measured lifespan in people. A 2025 study in Science from the National Institute on Aging found that blood taurine did not fall with age in humans, monkeys, or mice, which weakens the idea that you need to "top it up."

What is taurine, and why is it linked to aging?

Taurine is a sulfur-containing amino acid. Your body makes some of it, and you also get it from meat, fish, and shellfish. It is not used to build proteins. It works in bile, in cell volume control, and in the heart, eyes, and muscles. It is also a common ingredient in energy drinks.

The aging interest comes from one idea: taurine in the blood declines as we get older, and that decline may drive aging rather than just go along with it. If that were true, a supplement that restores youthful levels might slow aging. Each step of that chain needs evidence. The sections below show how far the evidence goes.

What did the 2023 mouse study find?

The paper that started the trend is "Taurine deficiency as a driver of aging" by Singh, Gollapalli, Yadav, and a large international team, published in Science in June 2023. The senior author, Vijay Yadav, is at Columbia University.

  • Mice. According to Columbia's summary of the study, the team started with close to 250 mice aged 14 months, roughly middle age. Half got taurine every day. Average lifespan rose 12% in females and 10% in males, or about three to four extra months.
  • Health span. Treated mice had less age-related weight gain, stronger bones and muscles, and better insulin sensitivity. At the cell level, taurine reduced senescent cells, DNA damage, and mitochondrial dysfunction.
  • Monkeys. Middle-aged rhesus monkeys took taurine for six months. They gained less weight and had lower fasting blood glucose and higher bone density. Lifespan was not measured in monkeys.
  • Humans. In about 12,000 European adults aged 60 and over, people with higher taurine levels had less type 2 diabetes, obesity, and hypertension. The authors noted that these are associations and do not establish cause.

The paper's own abstract ends by saying that clinical trials in humans "seem warranted." It does not claim that taurine extends human life. Columbia University also filed provisional patent applications on which Yadav is an inventor, as the paper's conflict statement discloses.

Do taurine levels really fall with age?

This is where the story got harder. The 2023 paper reported that blood taurine declines with age in mice, monkeys, and people. Two later studies did not find that pattern.

  • The 2025 NIA study. Fernandez, Bernier, Ferrucci, de Cabo, and colleagues at the National Institute on Aging published "Is taurine an aging biomarker?" in Science in June 2025. In three human groups from different countries, plus monkeys and mice, blood taurine rose or stayed the same with age. That held both when the same people were measured over time and when different age groups were compared. Links between taurine and health outcomes, such as motor function, were inconsistent.
  • The 2025 Montreal study. A study in Aging Cell measured taurine in 137 men aged 20 to 93, some active and some inactive. Taurine levels showed no link with age, muscle mass, strength, physical performance, or mitochondrial function.

The NIA authors concluded that changes in blood taurine "are not a universal feature of aging." That does not prove taurine supplements are useless. It does remove the simplest reason to take them: there may be no age-related shortage to fix.

What do human trials of taurine show?

Human trials have tested taurine for heart and metabolic risk factors, not for lifespan. Most were small and short.

  • A 2024 meta-analysis pooled 25 randomized trials with 1,024 people. Doses ranged from 0.5 to 6 grams per day, for 5 to 365 days. Taurine lowered systolic blood pressure by about 4 mmHg, fasting glucose by about 6 mg/dL, and triglycerides by about 18 mg/dL. The authors saw no significant adverse effects compared with control groups.
  • A larger 2025 meta-analysis in Nutrition Reviews pooled 34 randomized trials. It found small drops in fasting glucose, HbA1c, total and LDL cholesterol, triglycerides, blood pressure, and C-reactive protein. LDL fell by about 5 mg/dL. Effects looked strongest at 1.5 to 3 grams per day in the studies included.

These changes are real but modest. A 4 mmHg drop in systolic pressure is smaller than most blood pressure drugs achieve. Better lab numbers also do not prove a longer life. If high blood pressure is your concern, hypertension.md covers the treatments with outcome data behind them.

For comparison, the same pattern played out with NAD+ boosters. They raise a blood marker, but trials have not shown slower aging. We cover that in do NAD supplements slow aging?

Is taurine safe to take?

The safety record at studied amounts is good.

  • In 2009, the European Food Safety Authority concluded that taurine from regular energy drink consumption was not a safety concern. It set a no observed adverse effect level of 1,000 mg per kilogram of body weight per day. EFSA judged the ingredient, not energy drinks as a whole.
  • A 2008 risk assessment found strong evidence of no adverse effects at supplemental intakes up to 3 grams per day in healthy adults. Higher amounts have been tested, but the authors judged the long-term data too thin to call them safe with confidence.

Two cautions apply. First, energy drinks are not a taurine supplement. They also carry caffeine and sugar, which have their own limits. Second, supplements in the United States are not approved by the FDA before sale, so quality varies. See what third-party tested means before buying. Ask your doctor first if you take blood pressure or diabetes medicines, since taurine may lower both readings.

Is anyone testing taurine and aging in people?

No trial has measured whether taurine helps people live longer. Such a trial would take decades. Researchers instead use "biological age" markers as stand-ins.

The clearest example is a randomized, placebo-controlled trial at the Technical University of Munich. It gave 4 grams of taurine or placebo daily for 6 months to 90 adults aged 55 to 75. Its main outcomes are changes in biological age measured by blood proteins and DNA methylation. The registry lists it as completed in November 2025. We could not find published results as of October 2026.

Even a positive result would be a first step. Epigenetic clocks are research tools, and a slower clock is not the same as a longer life. For habits with stronger evidence for healthy aging, such as exercise, sleep, and not smoking, see thrive.md.

The bottom line

Taurine extended lifespan in mice in one well-known 2023 study. That is the only lifespan evidence, and no human study has tested it. Newer research suggests blood taurine does not reliably drop with age in people, which undercuts the main reason to supplement. In trials, taurine slightly improved blood pressure, glucose, and lipids, and it appears safe at studied amounts. Treat it as an unproven longevity idea, not an anti-aging pill.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is taurine the same thing as the energy drink ingredient?

Yes, it is the same molecule. Energy drinks add taurine alongside caffeine and sugar, so drinking them is not a clean way to test taurine. The caffeine and sugar carry their own health limits.

Do vegans and vegetarians get less taurine?

Taurine in food comes mainly from meat, fish, and shellfish, so plant-based diets supply little. The body also makes its own taurine. No study has shown that a low dietary intake shortens human life.

How long did mice in the Columbia study take taurine?

Mice started taurine at 14 months of age, roughly middle age, and took it daily. Health measures were taken after one year of supplementation. Lifespan was tracked for the rest of their lives.

Does exercise raise taurine levels?

The 2023 Science paper reported that blood taurine rose after a hard cycling session in athletes and sedentary people. That was a short-term rise after one workout. It does not show that exercise works through taurine.

Should I get my taurine level tested?

There is no accepted normal range that predicts aging or health in people. The 2025 NIA study found taurine did not track age in a consistent way. A blood test would not tell you whether a supplement would help.
