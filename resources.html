Resources

Trusted Supplement Resources

Curated list of reliable sources for researching supplements, checking quality, verifying interactions, and finding evidence-based information.

Government & Academic Resources

NIH Office of Dietary Supplements

The gold standard for supplement research. Provides comprehensive fact sheets on vitamins, minerals, and other supplements with evidence ratings, safety information, and research summaries. Start here for any supplement question.

Free Government

FDA Dietary Supplements

Official FDA information on supplement regulation, safety alerts, warning letters to companies, and recalled products. Good for checking if a product or company has received FDA warnings.

Free Government

NCCIH (NIH Complementary Health)

National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health. Excellent resource for herbal supplements, traditional remedies, and alternative medicine. Evidence-based reviews of popular herbs and natural products.

Free Government

Cochrane Library

Systematic reviews and meta-analyses of clinical evidence. When you want to know if a supplement actually works, Cochrane reviews compile and analyze all available research. Many reviews are free to access.

Free (most reviews) Academic

PubMed

Free database of biomedical literature. Search for clinical trials and research studies on any supplement. Tip: Add "systematic review" or "meta-analysis" to your search for higher-quality evidence summaries.

Free Academic

Third-Party Testing & Quality Verification

USP Verified Products Database

Search for supplements that have earned USP verification—the most rigorous third-party certification available. If your supplement is available with USP verification, it's the best quality choice.

Free Quality Testing

NSF Certified for Sport

Database of supplements certified free of banned substances. Required by NFL, MLB, NHL, PGA, and many college athletic programs. Good for athletes and anyone wanting rigorous testing.

Free Quality Testing

ConsumerLab

Independent testing company that purchases supplements off shelves and tests them without manufacturer cooperation. Detailed reviews compare products within categories. Excellent for finding quality products.

Subscription ~$50/year Quality Testing

Labdoor

Tests and grades popular supplements. Rankings based on label accuracy, purity, nutritional value, and ingredient safety. Free basic results; detailed reports require subscription.

Free (basic) Quality Testing

Interaction Checkers

Natural Medicines Database

The most comprehensive database for supplement-drug interactions, evidence ratings, and safety information. Used by healthcare professionals. Subscription required but very thorough.

Subscription Required Professional

Drugs.com Interaction Checker

Free interaction checker that includes some supplements. Good for quick checks, though not as comprehensive as Natural Medicines for supplements specifically.

Free Interactions

Memorial Sloan Kettering Herb Database

Excellent free resource from a major cancer center. Detailed information on herbal supplements including mechanisms, interactions, and safety concerns. Especially relevant for cancer patients.

Free Academic

Professional Organizations

American Academy of Family Physicians

Patient education materials on supplements and evidence-based guidance. Search their site for specific supplement topics.

Free Professional

Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics

Evidence-based nutrition information from registered dietitians. Good resource for understanding dietary sources vs. supplements.

Free Professional

US Preventive Services Task Force

Independent expert panel that reviews evidence for preventive services. Their recommendations on vitamin supplementation are highly influential and evidence-based.

Free Government

Safety Alerts & Recalls

FDA Recalls & Alerts

Official FDA recalls, market withdrawals, and safety alerts for dietary supplements. Check here if you're concerned about a specific product.

Free Government

Safe Medication (ISMP)

Consumer-focused medication safety information from the Institute for Safe Medication Practices. Includes supplement safety issues.

Free Safety

Your Healthcare Team

The most important resource is your own healthcare provider team:

  • Your doctor can order blood tests to check for deficiencies and assess whether a supplement is appropriate for your specific health situation
  • Your pharmacist is often the most accessible expert on drug interactions—they can check supplements against your medications
  • A registered dietitian can assess your diet and recommend whether supplements or dietary changes are more appropriate

Always bring a complete list of your supplements (including brands and doses) to medical appointments.
