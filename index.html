Supplements Guide

Evidence-based information on dietary supplements. What works, what doesn't, and how to stay safe.

⚠️ The Reality of Supplements

Supplements are NOT regulated like drugs. The FDA doesn't verify safety or effectiveness before they're sold. Claims don't require proof. Quality varies wildly between brands. "Natural" doesn't mean safe. Most people eating a balanced diet don't need supplements—but some do.

Evidence Ratings by Supplement

Strong Evidence

Vitamin D (if deficient)

Proven for bone health when deficient. Many people are deficient. Get tested first.

Strong Evidence

Folic Acid (pregnancy)

Prevents neural tube defects. All women who might become pregnant should take 400-800mcg daily.

Strong Evidence

B12 (if deficient)

Essential for vegans, older adults, those on metformin or PPIs. Deficiency causes serious problems.

Moderate Evidence

Fish Oil / Omega-3s

May reduce triglycerides and cardiovascular risk. Prescription versions work; OTC evidence is weaker.

Moderate Evidence

Magnesium

May help migraines, sleep, muscle cramps in some people. Many are mildly deficient.

Moderate Evidence

Creatine

Works for muscle strength and exercise performance. Well-studied and safe.

Weak/Mixed Evidence

Probiotics

May help specific conditions. Strain matters. Most general use has weak evidence.

Weak/Mixed Evidence

Vitamin C (colds)

Doesn't prevent colds. May slightly reduce duration if taken at onset. Megadoses don't help.

No Good Evidence

Multivitamins (general)

No proven benefit for most healthy adults eating varied diets. Won't compensate for poor diet.

No Good Evidence

Detox / Cleanse Products

Your liver and kidneys detox you. These products don't do anything useful.

Safety Concerns

🚨 Supplements Can Be Dangerous

  • Drug interactions: St. John's Wort, ginkgo, garlic can dangerously interact with medications
  • Contamination: Some supplements contain unlisted ingredients, heavy metals, or drugs
  • Overdose: Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) can accumulate to toxic levels
  • Surgery risk: Many supplements affect bleeding—stop before surgery
  • Pregnancy: Many are unsafe during pregnancy
