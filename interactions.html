Drug Interactions

Supplement-Drug Interactions

Supplements can interact with prescription and over-the-counter medications in ways that reduce drug effectiveness or increase side effects. Here are the most important interactions to know.

⚠️ Always Tell Your Doctor

Inform your healthcare provider about ALL supplements you take, including vitamins, minerals, herbs, and "natural" products. Bring the bottles to appointments. Some interactions can be serious or even life-threatening. Never stop a prescribed medication to take a supplement instead without medical guidance.

High-Risk Interactions

These interactions can be dangerous and require careful management or avoidance:

Supplement Interacts With Effect Recommendation
St. John's Wort Birth control pills, SSRIs, HIV medications, warfarin, cyclosporine, many others Reduces drug levels via CYP450 enzyme induction; can cause treatment failure or serotonin syndrome Avoid combination; tell doctor immediately if taking
Vitamin K Warfarin (Coumadin) Reduces anticoagulant effect; can cause blood clots Keep vitamin K intake consistent; don't suddenly increase leafy greens or supplements
Ginkgo biloba Blood thinners (warfarin, aspirin, clopidogrel) Increases bleeding risk Avoid combination; stop 2 weeks before surgery
High-dose Vitamin E Blood thinners, chemotherapy drugs Increased bleeding; may reduce chemo effectiveness Avoid high doses (>400 IU) with these medications
Grapefruit (juice/extract) Statins, calcium channel blockers, some immunosuppressants, many drugs Increases drug levels to potentially toxic amounts Avoid grapefruit entirely with affected medications

Moderate Interactions

These require attention and timing adjustments but can often be managed:

Supplement Interacts With Effect Recommendation
Calcium Thyroid medications (levothyroxine), tetracycline antibiotics, bisphosphonates Reduces absorption of the medication Take calcium 4+ hours apart from these medications
Iron Thyroid medications, antibiotics (quinolones, tetracyclines), levodopa Reduces absorption of the medication Take iron 2-4 hours apart from these medications
Magnesium Antibiotics, bisphosphonates, diuretics Reduces drug absorption; diuretics can deplete magnesium Separate doses by 2+ hours; monitor if on diuretics
Fish oil (high dose) Blood thinners, blood pressure medications May increase bleeding risk; may enhance BP-lowering effect Monitor; inform doctor of high-dose fish oil use
Fiber supplements Many oral medications Can reduce or delay drug absorption Take medications 1 hour before or 2 hours after fiber
Potassium ACE inhibitors, ARBs, potassium-sparing diuretics Risk of hyperkalemia (high potassium) Avoid potassium supplements; monitor blood levels

Interactions by Drug Class

Blood Thinners (Warfarin, Aspirin, Clopidogrel)

Blood thinners are among the most interaction-prone medications. Supplements that can increase bleeding risk:

  • Increase bleeding: Fish oil, vitamin E (high dose), ginkgo, garlic, ginger, feverfew, dong quai, willow bark
  • Decrease effectiveness: Vitamin K, St. John's Wort, coenzyme Q10

Diabetes Medications

  • May lower blood sugar further: Chromium, alpha-lipoic acid, berberine, fenugreek, bitter melon
  • B12 with metformin: Metformin reduces B12 absorption; supplementation may be needed

Blood Pressure Medications

  • May lower BP further: Coenzyme Q10, fish oil, garlic, potassium
  • May increase potassium: Potassium supplements with ACE inhibitors/ARBs

Thyroid Medications (Levothyroxine)

Many supplements reduce levothyroxine absorption:

  • Calcium, iron, magnesium, aluminum (antacids)
  • Take thyroid medication on an empty stomach, 4 hours before or after these supplements

Antidepressants (SSRIs, MAOIs)

  • Serotonin syndrome risk: St. John's Wort, 5-HTP, SAMe, tryptophan with SSRIs
  • MAOIs: Tyramine-containing supplements, ginseng

St. John's Wort: The Most Dangerous Supplement

St. John's Wort deserves special attention because it interacts with an exceptionally wide range of medications. It induces CYP450 enzymes in the liver, which metabolize many drugs faster than intended.

Medications significantly affected by St. John's Wort:

  • Birth control pills (risk of unintended pregnancy)
  • HIV medications (protease inhibitors, NNRTIs)
  • Immunosuppressants (cyclosporine, tacrolimus)
  • Warfarin and other anticoagulants
  • Antidepressants (SSRIs, SNRIs)
  • Statins
  • Calcium channel blockers
  • Benzodiazepines
  • Opioid pain medications
  • Cancer medications

⚠️ If You Take St. John's Wort

Tell your doctor immediately. Do not start St. John's Wort without checking interactions with all your current medications. Do not stop taking it suddenly if you're on other medications—the abrupt change can also be dangerous.

Before Surgery

Many supplements should be stopped before surgical procedures due to bleeding risk or anesthesia interactions:

Stop at least 2 weeks before surgery:

  • Fish oil / omega-3s
  • Vitamin E (high dose)
  • Ginkgo biloba
  • Garlic supplements
  • Ginger (high dose)
  • Feverfew
  • St. John's Wort
  • Ginseng
  • Kava
  • Valerian

💡 Tell Your Surgical Team

Bring a complete list of all supplements to your pre-operative appointment. Anesthesiologists need this information to ensure safe anesthesia and avoid interactions during surgery.

Resources for Checking Interactions

Use these tools to check for interactions, but always confirm with your healthcare provider:

  • Natural Medicines Database: Most comprehensive (subscription required)
  • NIH Office of Dietary Supplements: Free fact sheets with interaction info
  • Drugs.com Interaction Checker: Free, includes some supplements
  • Your pharmacist: Often the most accessible expert on drug interactions
