Supplement Drug Interactions

Updated Feb 2026 · 10 min read

"Natural" doesn't mean safe to combine with medications. Some supplements have serious, even life-threatening interactions with drugs.

High-Risk Supplements

🚨 St. John's Wort

The biggest offender. Speeds up drug metabolism, making many medications less effective:

  • Birth control pills (can cause pregnancy)
  • Blood thinners (warfarin)
  • HIV medications
  • Antidepressants (risk of serotonin syndrome)
  • Immunosuppressants (organ rejection risk)
  • Many cancer drugs

🚨 Ginkgo Biloba

Increases bleeding risk. Dangerous with:

  • Blood thinners (warfarin, aspirin, Plavix)
  • NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen)
  • Before surgery (stop 2 weeks before)

🚨 Fish Oil / Omega-3s (high doses)

Increases bleeding risk at high doses (above 3g/day):

  • Blood thinners
  • Aspirin
  • Before surgery

🚨 Vitamin K

Directly counteracts warfarin. If you take warfarin, keep vitamin K intake consistent. Don't start or stop supplements without telling your doctor.

🚨 Calcium

Blocks absorption of many drugs:

  • Thyroid medication (levothyroxine)—take 4 hours apart
  • Certain antibiotics (fluoroquinolones, tetracyclines)
  • Bisphosphonates (osteoporosis drugs)

🚨 Iron

Similar absorption issues:

  • Thyroid medication—take 4 hours apart
  • Certain antibiotics
  • Levodopa (Parkinson's)

Before Surgery

Many supplements affect bleeding or anesthesia. Tell your surgeon about EVERYTHING you take. Common ones to stop 1-2 weeks before surgery:

  • Fish oil / omega-3s
  • Vitamin E (high doses)
  • Ginkgo
  • Garlic supplements
  • Ginger (high doses)
  • St. John's Wort
  • Ginseng

✅ How to Check for Interactions

Tell your doctor and pharmacist about all supplements. Use drug interaction checkers like drugs.com or Medscape. The NIH's "MedlinePlus" has interaction information. When in doubt, ask before combining.

The Golden Rule

Always tell every healthcare provider about every supplement you take. Bring the bottles to appointments. Pharmacists are excellent resources for checking interactions—and it's free to ask them.
